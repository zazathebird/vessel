They were flat because they were wire.
Two changes, same costumes. The body is a mass: every bone is a tapered capsule, wide at the root and narrow at the tip, with the torso a shape between the shoulders and the hips. A stroke has no width that changes, so it has no direction, and a limb with no direction is a wire. Every shape carries its own edge: each one is laid down twice, first in the palette’s background role at a wider line, then in ink — so a helmet stops where the skull starts, and the near leg crosses in front of the far one. The second tone is a palette role, not a literal, so it cross-fades with the site and holds on all twenty-five palettes.
Left of each pair is the current build. Right is the same costume, same stance, same proportions.

Why they went
Each one was either a second copy of a stronger silhouette — three brimmed hats, four blocks for a head, two capes to the floor — or a costume whose whole read was interior detail: a sash, a bead loop, a bandage, a bird. Interior detail is the first thing to go at 61px, which is where this actually renders.
