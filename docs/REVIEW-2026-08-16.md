### 2. ~~HIGH~~ — FIXED 2026-08-16: the tablet band sliced "Guestbook" mid-word

At **600px** the pill row overflows by **48px** and the last pill renders as `GUEST` with a hard
edge. That is precisely the symptom the 2026-08-14 audit found on the phone band and fixed with a
34px trailing `mask-image` fade — *"a cut letter reads as broken, a fading one reads as
continuing"*.

The fade was then deliberately scoped to `.band-phone` only (`chrome.css:296`), on the stated
premise that a tablet *"fits the six public pills with room to spare"*. **That premise died when
Scams became a seventh pill** later the same day, and the tablet band was never re-measured.

| width | band | overflow | fade | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 600 | tablet | 48px | **no** | **"Guestbook" sliced** |
| 760 | tablet | 0 | n/a | fits |
| 880 | tablet | 0 | n/a | fits |
| 1280 | desk | 0 | n/a | wraps |

Affected range ≈ **560–710px**: a phone in landscape, a small tablet in portrait.

**Fixed as recommended**: the fade is now bound to a measurement, not a breakpoint.
`src/hooks/useEdgeFade.ts` writes `data-fade` on the row and `chrome.css` masks off that attribute;
**no band appears in the selector at all**. A row that does not scroll gets no attribute and no
mask, which is what the phone-only scoping was protecting — an affordance pointing at nothing is
worse than none.

Deliberately *not* done: adding `.band-tablet` to the old selector. At 760px and up there is nothing
to fade, so that would have reinstated the exact bug the original note describes deleting — and it
would have failed again on the operator's **ten**-pill nav, which no breakpoint list covers.

**A third instance of the same bug was found while fixing it, and is also fixed.** Scrolled to the
end of the phone row, the trailing fade still pointed right at nothing, while `About` sat sliced
flat against the *left* edge with two pills hidden behind it. One edge was lying in every scrolled
state. The mask now fades whichever edges genuinely hide something (`start` / `end` / `both`).

**Verified**: `data-fade` and the mask present at 390/420 (phone) and 600 (tablet); **absent** at
700/880 (tablet, fits) and 1280 (desk, wraps). CSS confirmed for all three attribute values.

**What could not be verified in a browser here, stated rather than glossed**: the transitions
*between* scroll states. This tab reports `document.hidden`, so `requestAnimationFrame` correctly
parks — measured, zero callbacks in 900ms — and the scrolled states are unreachable. So the decision
is a pure exported function, `edgeState(scrollLeft, scrollWidth, clientWidth)`, on the same argument
that made `duelCamera` pure: what cannot be observed here gets stepped through in Node instead.
**13 cases pass**, including both dead bands, the measured phone and tablet geometries, overscroll,
and the ten-pill operator nav.

### 3. MEDIUM — on a phone, Contact and Guestbook are both entirely off-screen

The row scrolls and the fade advertises it, so nothing is broken. But the fade was sized against a
**66px** overflow; with seven pills it is **212–242px**, and the two pills past the edge are Contact
and Guestbook.

| width | overflow | fully hidden | cut |
|---|---|---|---|
| 390 | 242px | **Contact, Guestbook** | Gallery |
| 420 | 212px | **Contact, Guestbook** | Gallery |

Contact survives via the home hero's *Fix my computer* CTA. From any other page on a phone, reaching
it means discovering that a row of buttons scrolls sideways.

**Options** (cheapest first): leave it; widen the fade so more of the next pill peeks; or **move
Contact earlier in `NAV`** so the page with a job is never the hidden one. Recommend the last — it
costs nothing and puts ordering where the priority already is. Note `NAV` order is what
`useOperatorRoutes` cycles and what Radial's orbit renders, so a reorder moves both; that is
cosmetic, not structural.
